Portfolio Framework

Project Overview & Analytics Methodology

A standardized 7-stage analytical lifecycle executed across 5 industry sectors
Standardized Workflow: 1. Data Ingestion  →  2. Cleaning  →  3. Validation & Unit Tests  →  4. Statistical EDA  →  5. Publication Visuals  →  6. Insights  →  7. Testable Recommendations
Five Multi-Domain Datasets
  • Supermarket Sales: 2,000 retail transactions; branch revenue, basket size, time-period traffic, and customer ratings.
  • Student Performance: 500 student academic records; study hours, attendance %, subject scores, and performance tiers.
  • Weather Analysis: 365 daily station records; annual rainfall concentration, temperature cycles, and extreme conditions.
  • COVID Healthcare: 1,825 regional tracking logs; case waves, vaccination phases, and regional mortality disparities.
  • Stock Market Risk: 252 trading days; compounded return, price volatility, max drawdown, and Sharpe ratio.
Statistical Analysis & Rigor
  • Central Tendency: Mean, median, and quartile analysis to detect skewness and avoid outlier distortions.
  • Dispersion & Risk: Standard deviation, interquartile range (IQR), annualized volatility, and maximum drawdown.
  • Correlation Analysis: Pearson correlation matrices with strict distinction between correlation and causation.
  • Stratification: Performance tier segmentation, customer classification, and vaccination phase stratification.
  • Time-Series Profiling: 7-day moving averages, seasonal aggregation, and month-over-month compounding.
Engineering & Quality Stack
  • Python Core Stack: Python 3.13, Pandas 2.2.0, NumPy 1.24.0, SciPy 1.12.0 for numerical computing.
  • Visualization Engine: Matplotlib 3.8.0 and Seaborn 0.13.0 for accessible, labeled figures.
  • Executive Reporting: FPDF2 2.7.8 for programmatic generation of formal PDF reports.
  • Automated Testing: Pytest framework verifying shapes, nulls, duplicates, and formulas (18 tests passed).
  • Integrity Principle: Zero artificial smoothing; raw source anomalies documented as limitations.

Academic Insights & Student Segmentation

Meteorological Patterns & Seasonal Observations
  • Seasonal Temperature Shifts: Spring is warmest (avg 38.07°C, max 46.4°C), Summer moderate (avg 22.15°C), Winter cool (avg 27.72°C), and Autumn coldest (avg 12.10°C, min 0.6°C). Peak-to-trough spread is ~26°C.
  • Monsoon Rainfall Concentration: 77 heavy-rain days (>10 mm) produce 1,583.00 mm (~65% of total annual rain), concentrated in June–September (June: 406.8mm, July: 328.5mm, Aug: 408.6mm, Sept: 408.9mm).

  • Weak Cross-Variable Linear Correlation: Pairwise correlations between temperature, humidity, wind, and rainfall were weak (|r| < 0.15), reflecting non-linear atmospheric dynamics.
Scientific Limitation: Single-Station Observation
This analysis describes empirical observations from 365 days of 2023 data from a single station. A single year of data is insufficient to establish a formal long-term climate classification or multi-decadal trend under WMO standards.

Epidemiological Patterns & Regional Disparities
  • Three Pandemic Wave Periods: Period 1 (Feb–Mar): Peak 57,108 cases in Feb. Period 2 (May–Jun): Annual peak 87,244 cases in May. Period 3 (Sept–Oct): Muted peak 35,377 cases in Oct. Dec low was 2,631.
  • Vaccination Phase Association: High vaccination phases (60%+ coverage) averaged 101.28 daily cases vs 234.17 in low vaccination phases (0–30%), representing an observed 56.7% lower observed daily cases in the high-vaccination phase.
  • Regional Mortality Disparities: Central recorded 1,118 deaths (24.8% of total) vs East 787 deaths (17.5%). Central had 42.1% more deaths despite identical average vaccination rates (47.98%).
  • Correlation Nuances: Vaccination rate vs Daily Cases showed moderate negative correlation (r = -0.3574); vaccination vs Deaths was weak (r = -0.1604); Daily Cases vs Deaths was positive (r = +0.3433).
  • Healthcare Capacity Implications: Mortality variation was substantially higher than vaccination differences, pointing to regional variations in ICU beds, hospital capacity, and age demographics.
Methodological Note: Distinguishing Association from Causation
The 56.7% case difference across vaccination phases is an observed statistical association. Observational aggregate data cannot establish singular clinical causation, as transmission was concurrently influenced by variants, mandates, and mobility.

Price Action, Return Profiles & Risk Metrics
  • Price Range & Rebound: Lowest close reached $80.41 on May 26; price rallied 46.4% to a peak close of $117.74 on Nov 6, closing the period at $111.30 (+10.14% period return).
  • Quarterly Trajectory: Severe early weakness in Q1 (-18.67% compounded, with Feb -9.79% worst month), followed by rebound in Q2 (+8.89%), Q3 (+7.68%), and Q4 (+15.49%, led by Oct +17.91% best month).

Data Quality Limitation: OHLC Data Inconsistencies
The dataset contains 78 rows with OHLC consistency issues (40 Open > High, 38 Open < Low). High >= Low had 0 violations. Return formulas were mathematically validated (<1e-13% diff). Original data retained without alteration.

Strategic Action Plan

Cross-Domain Business Recommendations

Translating empirical data findings into testable operational adjustments, resource allocations, and monitored KPIs
01 Supermarket Sales Optimization Target KPIs: Hourly queue times, Branch C revenue gap, low-rating %
Empirical Finding: 42.2% of transactions occur in the afternoon (12-5 PM); Branch C lags Branch B by 4.1%; 18.7% give low ratings.
Strategic Action: Reallocate staff & open additional checkout counters from 12-5 PM; conduct audit into Branch C footfall and marketing; investigate root causes of low customer satisfaction.
02 Student Academic Support Target KPIs: Diagnostic scores, tier transition rates, concept gap reduction
Empirical Finding: Study hours (r=0.096) and attendance (r=0.077) show very weak linear correlation with grades; effort between tiers differs by <3%.
Strategic Action: Move away from rigid study-hour mandates; implement diagnostic assessments early to identify specific concept gaps; provide personalized learning and remediation.
03 Municipal Water & Infrastructure Planning Target KPIs: Rainwater capture volume, flood incident frequency, construction delay days
Empirical Finding: 64.7% of annual rainfall (1,583 mm) falls in 77 days during June-September; peak daily rainfall reached 86.4 mm.
Strategic Action: Develop rainwater harvesting infrastructure before June; design storm drainage to 86.4 mm capacity; pause weather-sensitive outdoor construction during monsoon.
04 Healthcare Resource Allocation Target KPIs: Regional Case Fatality Rate (CFR), ICU occupancy rate, local vaccine coverage
Empirical Finding: Central region recorded 42.1% excess mortality over East despite identical average vaccination rates (47.98%).
Strategic Action: Prioritize clinical ICU and ventilator resources to Central region; deploy targeted mobile vaccine clinics to low-coverage pockets; audit hospital capacity disparities.
05 Quantitative Risk Management Target KPIs: Trailing volatility, max peak-to-trough drawdown, benchmark-adjusted Sharpe
Empirical Finding: Stock delivered +10.14% return but suffered -26.85% maximum drawdown and 30.87% annualized volatility (Sharpe 0.47).
Strategic Action: Treat $80-$85 as historical reference rather than guaranteed support; implement risk management rules to limit drawdowns; balance return against volatility.
